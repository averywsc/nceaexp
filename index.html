Core System Explained

How NCEA Works at WSCW

NCEA stands for the National Certificate of Educational Achievement. Instead of getting one percentage out of 100 for a whole subject at the end of the year, every subject is broken into smaller topics called Standards, and each standard is worth a set number of credits.

The 60-Credit Rule and WSCW's 3-Year Structure

To earn NCEA at any level, you need 60 credits at that level (or above) plus the one-off 20-credit Literacy and Numeracy Co-requisite.

Year 11 at WSCW

Two Distinct Pathways

Springs (Mainstream): Students take 6 subjects (English, Maths, Science, plus 3 options) on a 2-year NCEA Level 2 pathway. Most Year 11 courses offer one Level 2 standard (2 to 6 credits), letting you bank roughly 20 to 30 Level 2 credits early while sitting the Co-requisite.

Ngā Puna o Waiōrea: Students work toward a full NCEA Level 1 certificate (60 Level 1 credits across Pāngarau, Pūtaiao, Te Reo Māori or Te Reo Rangatira, Te Reo Pākehā, and options).

Year 12 (Level 2)

60 Level 2 Credits

Students take English (or Te Reo Pākehā / ESOL) plus 5 optional subjects (4 options for Waiōrea). Courses offer between 14 and 24 credits each.

Any Level 2 credits you earned in Year 11 count automatically toward your 60-credit NCEA Level 2 total. Level 2 is the key benchmark for apprenticeships, trades, and polytechnics.

Year 13 (Level 3)

60 Level 3 Credits

Students choose 5 subjects (English is required if you have not yet completed UE Literacy). Every Level 3 course at WSCW offers over 14 credits.

Entry into most Level 3 courses requires 12 Level 2 credits in that subject (or HOD approval). Passing 60 Level 3 credits awards NCEA Level 3, which is step one for University Entrance.

Note for Whānau Who Remember the Older 80-Credit Rule

Previously, NCEA required 80 credits at Level 1 and 60 credits plus 20 carried-over credits at Levels 2 and 3. Under the current system, there are no 20 carry-over credits. Every level requires 60 credits at that level (or above), and the 20-credit Literacy and Numeracy Co-requisite sits in its own separate bucket.

The 20-Credit Literacy and Numeracy Co-Requisite

You must pass this once during high school to be awarded NCEA Level 1, 2, or 3. Most WSCW students start sitting these digital tests in Year 10 or Year 11.

5 Credits

Reading

US32403 (English) or US32413 (Te Reo Matatini)

Understand everyday written texts, locate key ideas, and evaluate information for reliability and purpose.

5 Credits

Writing

US32405 (English) or US32415 (Te Reo Matatini)

Write clear, accurate responses for real-world audiences using sound structure, grammar, and punctuation.

10 Credits

Numeracy

US32406 (Maths) or US32412 (Pāngarau)

Apply everyday maths and statistics (percentages, budgets, graphs, measurement, probability) to practical problems.

How the Co-Requisite Assessments (CAAs) Work

  • Digital online tests: Sat at school through NZQA's digital assessment platform during Term 2 and Term 3 windows.
  • Unlimited retries: If you do not pass one component on your first try, you simply re-sit that part in the next assessment window.
  • Achieved or Not Achieved only: There are no Merit or Excellence grades, so co-requisite credits never count toward Endorsements.
  • Separate from your 60 credits: These 20 credits unlock your NCEA certificates, rather than filling the 60 subject credits.

Knowing which type of standard you are taking tells you what grades are possible and whether it counts toward Endorsements or University Entrance.

Internal Assessments

Completed in class or at home during the year: essays, lab investigations, speeches, performances, coding projects, or design folios. Marked by your WSCW teachers and checked nationwide through NZQA External Moderation so grading stays fair across all schools.

External Assessments

Marked independently by NZQA outside of school. Most are 3-hour November exams (on paper or digitally on laptops), while Art Design, Painting, Photography, DVC, and Technology use externally marked folio boards or submitted reports. Results are released online in mid-January.

Grades and the 0 to 8 Exam Marking Scale

NCEA is standards-based, not curved. Higher grades do not give you extra credits; they improve the quality of those credits for Endorsements and University Rank Scores.

0 credits

Not Achieved (N)

Did not yet meet the standard criteria. Does not subtract from your other credits.

2 Rank pts / cr

Achieved (A)

Solid grasp of the core skills. Full credits awarded. Typically asks you to describe or apply.

3 Rank pts / cr

Merit (M)

In-depth understanding with clear reasoning. Typically asks you to explain in depth, justify, or analyse.

4 Rank pts / cr

Excellence (E)

Comprehensive, perceptive mastery. Typically asks you to evaluate, compare, or synthesise.

How External Exam Scores Work (N0 to E8)

External exams do not use percentages. Each question (usually 3 questions per standard) is given a score from N0 to E8. Click any score below to see what it means:

E7 (Low Excellence, 7 points): Comprehensive, Excellence-level work on this question with minor slips or a small omission.

How the final paper grade is decided: The three question scores add up to a total out of 24. NZQA sets annual Cut Scores for each exam (commonly around 0 to 7 = NA, 8 to 13 = A, 14 to 18 = M, and 19 to 24 = E). That means getting E7 + M6 + M6 = 19/24 can earn an overall Excellence grade for the whole standard.

Certificate & Course Endorsements

How Endorsements Work

Endorsements recognise consistently high achievement on your official NZQA Record of Achievement. There are two completely separate types of endorsement: Certificate Endorsements (for your whole level) and Course Endorsements (for an individual subject).

Whole NCEA Level

1. Certificate Endorsement

Recognises strong overall results across all your subjects at Level 1, Level 2, or Level 3.

  • Excellence Endorsement: Earn at least 50 credits at Excellence at that level or above.
  • Merit Endorsement: Earn at least 50 credits at Merit (or Merit + Excellence combined) at that level or above.
  • Works across multiple years: At WSCW, Level 2 Merit or Excellence credits you earn in Year 11 automatically add to your Year 12 credits toward your Level 2 Certificate Endorsement.
  • No internal/external split needed: Any mix of internal and external Achievement Standards counts.
Single Subject

2. Course Endorsement

Recognises strong achievement inside one specific subject (for example, Level 2 Biology Endorsed with Excellence).

  • 14+ Credits Rule: Earn at least 14 credits in that subject at the endorsement grade (or higher).
  • Three levels available: Courses can be endorsed with Achieved, Merit, or Excellence.
  • Single calendar year only: All 14 credits must be gained in the same school year. Year 11 Level 2 credits cannot combine with Year 12 credits for a Course Endorsement.
  • The 3 Internal + 3 External Rule: At least 3 credits must be Internal and at least 3 credits must be External, both at the endorsement grade level (for Excellence endorsement, you need at least 3 internal E credits and at least 3 external E credits).

Important for WSCW Art and PE Students

At WSCW, Level 2 and Level 3 Physical Education / Hākinakina are 100% internal, and NZQA explicitly exempts PE from the 3-external rule so you can still get Merit or Excellence Course Endorsement. Similarly, Level 3 Art Design, Art Painting, and Art Photography are exempt and can be endorsed via their 14-credit external folio board alone. Subjects like Level 2/3 Music, Design Technology, and Food Technology at WSCW are internal-only and are not on NZQA's exemption list, so while their Merit and Excellence credits count fully toward your Certificate Endorsement and Rank Score, they cannot earn a Course Endorsement without 3 external credits.

      Tertiary & Career Pathways

      University Entrance, Rank Scores, and Scholarship

      University Entrance (UE) is the minimum requirement to enrol in a New Zealand university degree. Competitive degrees also require a specific Rank Score calculated from your best 80 Level 3 credits.

      The 4 Requirements for University Entrance (UE)

      Requirement 1

      NCEA Level 3

      Earn the full NCEA Level 3 qualification (60 Level 3 credits plus the 20-credit Co-requisite).

      Requirement 2

      3 Approved Subjects

      Earn at least 14 credits at Level 3 in each of three UE-approved subjects.

      Requirement 3

      UE Literacy

      10 credits at Level 2 or above from NZQA's UE Literacy list: 5 Reading + 5 Writing.

      Requirement 4

      UE Numeracy

      10 credits at Level 1 or above. Passing the Numeracy Co-requisite (US32406 / 32412) ticks this off.

      Common Trap: NCEA Literacy Is Not UE Literacy

      Passing the Level 1 Reading and Writing Co-requisite unlocks NCEA Levels 1, 2, and 3, but it does not give you UE Literacy. You still need 5 Reading and 5 Writing credits from Level 2 or Level 3 UE Literacy standards (such as WSCW's Level 2 English, Classics, History, Art History, Health, Economics, or Biology). You can check exact UE Reading and Writing credits on every subject inside the WSCW Courses tab.

        New Zealand Scholarship (Year 13)

        WSCW offers Scholarship preparation classes in Year 13. Scholarship exams sit above Level 3, award no NCEA credits, and provide cash awards for university study in New Zealand.

        Award Value How You Win It
        Premier Award $10,000 per year (up to 3 years, $30,000 total) Top 7 to 13 students in New Zealand (usually 3+ Outstanding Scholarships plus high overall results).
        Outstanding Scholar $5,000 per year (up to 3 years, $15,000 total) Next top 40 to 60 students nationally (e.g. 3 Scholarships with 2+ Outstanding, or 5+ Scholarships including 1 Outstanding).
        Top Subject Scholar $2,000 per year (up to 3 years, $6,000 total) Highest-scoring candidate in New Zealand for a single Scholarship subject.

        Multi-year monetary awards require full-time tertiary study in New Zealand and maintaining at least a B grade average each year.

        Assessment Rules & Reference

        Safety Nets, Policies, and NCEA Terms

        Clear answers to the most important NZQA and WSCW rules around resubmissions, mock exams, special assessment conditions, future reforms, and everyday acronyms.

        Essential Assessment Rules

        Resubmission (NA to Achieved only): Offered only when your internal assessment sits on the borderline between Not Achieved and Achieved, letting you fix a small specific mistake without further teaching. Under NZQA rules, the maximum grade from a resubmission is Achieved. You cannot resubmit to move from Achieved to Merit or Merit to Excellence.

        Further Assessment Opportunity (FAO): A brand-new assessment task for the same standard after additional teaching. Schools can offer at most one FAO per standard per year (not all standards offer one). Unlike a resubmission, an FAO allows the full range of grades up to Excellence.

        If acute illness, injury, bereavement, or approved national representation stops you sitting a November external exam (or severely affects your performance), you can apply for a Derived Grade.

        NZQA only approves Derived Grades supported by verified, standard-specific school evidence, which almost always comes from your Term 3 Practice Exams. Treating WSCW practice exams seriously gives you an essential safety net if anything goes wrong in November. Note that Derived Grades cannot be used for externally submitted portfolios or NZ Scholarship.

        Students with diagnosed learning, sensory, medical, or physical barriers (such as dyslexia, ADHD, anxiety disorders, or visual impairments) can apply through WSCW Learning Support for SAC. Support includes extra time, a reader or writer, computer use, rest breaks, or a separate room so you can show what you know fairly.

        When external results arrive in January, you can check your marked papers and apply (typically by late February) for either a Review (free check for clerical or addition errors, without re-marking) or a Reconsideration (small fee, refunded if your grade increases, where a senior marker completely re-marks your paper). Always review your paper with your teacher before applying.

        Per WSCW's official course books, fully STAR-funded courses are funded only for New Zealand residents. International students are not eligible for Performing Arts Technology (PAT), Gateway, Sports Academy, the First Aid component of Year 12 Health, or the Year 13 university Philosophy paper. Check with the WSCW International Department before locking in selections.

        The New Zealand Government has announced plans to gradually replace NCEA with a subject-based system featuring a Year 11 Foundational Award, a Year 12 New Zealand Certificate of Education (NZCE, from around 2028), and a Year 13 New Zealand Advanced Certificate of Education (NZACE, from around 2029/2030) graded from A+ to E.

        Crucial reassurance: Nobody has to switch systems mid-stream. Students currently in senior school at WSCW finish under the NCEA framework explained in this guide, and all NCEA qualifications remain fully valid and recognised.
